Motivated by the recent realization of goldene, two-dimensional metallic kagome lattices present an ideal platform for exploring geometric frustration and lattice stability in elemental systems. [cite_start]This work explores free-standing kagome monolayers of Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), and Gold (Au)[cite: 1671, 1677].

**Key Findings:**
* **Stability and Strain Engineering**: While all systems satisfy 2D Born stability criteria, unstrained kagome phases are dynamically unstable. [cite_start]However, moderate biaxial tensile strain successfully stabilizes Ag and Au kagome monolayers, but fails for Cu[cite: 1671, 1677].
* [cite_start]**Mechanical Properties**: The kagome topology leads to inherently low stiffness and strong lattice softening[cite: 1671, 1677].
* [cite_start]**Finite-Temperature Behavior**: Simulations reveal competing kagome and trigonal phases for Au, metastability for Ag, and rapid reconstruction in Cu[cite: 1671, 1677].

**Methodology:**
[cite_start]The structural, mechanical, and thermal properties were evaluated through extensive first-principles calculations (density functional theory) and *ab initio* molecular dynamics (AIMD)[cite: 1671, 1677].
