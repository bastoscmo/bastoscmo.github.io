Janus MXenes represent a promising class of two-dimensional materials featuring tunable electronic and optical properties that arise from their intrinsic structural asymmetry. [cite_start]This study investigates Janus $\text{Sc}_2\text{CTT}^\prime$ and $\text{Y}_2\text{CTT}^\prime$ monolayers ($\text{T=Br, Cl, F; T}^\prime\text{=H}$)[cite: 2343, 2349].

**Key Findings:**
* **Stability & Mechanics**: The materials are highly stable, as confirmed by cohesive energies, phonon dispersions, and AIMD. [cite_start]They also exhibit nearly isotropic mechanical responses[cite: 2343, 2349].
* [cite_start]**Electronic Structure**: These MXenes present indirect band gaps ranging from 1.7 eV to 1.9 eV (HSE06), driven by strong orbital contributions from transition-metal *d* states[cite: 2343, 2349].
* **Optoelectronic & Photovoltaic Performance**: The Bethe-Salpeter equation reveals robust excitonic binding energies up to 0.49 eV and broad absorption spectra (visible to UV). [cite_start]Notably, power conversion efficiencies can reach values above 32% for Y-based Janus MXenes[cite: 2343, 2349].

**Methodology:**
The study combined Density Functional Theory (DFT) with a maximally localized Wannier-based tight-binding approach. [cite_start]Excitonic effects were captured using the Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE), and solar efficiency was evaluated using Shockley-Queisser and spectroscopy-limited models[cite: 2343, 2349].
