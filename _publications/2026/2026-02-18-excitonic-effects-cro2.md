The development of new 2D materials has expanded the frontiers for emerging technologies in photovoltaics and optoelectronics. [cite_start]In this work, we address a significant gap by systematically investigating the properties of the $\text{CrO}_2$ monolayer in the 2H phase[cite: 200, 203].

**Key Findings:**
* **Stability**: The material is dynamically stable. [cite_start]Quasi-harmonic phonon calculations indicate that the 2H phase is thermodynamically stabilized only for temperatures below 660 K[cite: 200, 203].
* [cite_start]**Electronic Structure**: The monolayer is an indirect band gap semiconductor (0.93 eV), with a direct band gap of 1.84 eV located in the $\text{K/K}^\prime$ valleys[cite: 200, 203].
* **Excitonic and Optical Properties**: Excitonic effects play a crucial role, presenting an exciton binding energy of 386 meV. [cite_start]This leads to an indirect excitonic ground state and an optical band gap of 1.20 eV, resulting in high reflectivity[cite: 200, 203].

**Methodology:**
[cite_start]The study combined first-principles calculations to evaluate structural, electronic, excitonic, and optical properties, utilizing quasi-harmonic phonon analysis for thermodynamics and solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation for many-body effects[cite: 200, 203].
