Two-dimensional MXenes have emerged as a prominent family of materials due to their remarkable tunable properties through surface functionalization. In this study, we provide a detailed investigation into how different functional groups influence the structural stability and the excitonic response of these monolayers, specifically for photovoltaic applications.

**Key Findings:**
* **Surface Functionalization**: The study demonstrates that surface termination significantly modifies the electronic band structure, allowing for precise control over the material's metallic or semiconducting behavior.
* **Excitonic Insights**: We identified strong electron-hole interactions in the functionalized phases, with binding energies that play a critical role in determining the optical absorption spectrum.
* **Photovoltaic Potential**: The analysis of the solar power conversion efficiency reveals that specific functionalized MXenes meet the criteria for high-performance thin-film solar cells.
* **Structural Stability**: All investigated structures were verified for dynamical stability, ensuring their potential for experimental synthesis and integration into devices.

**Methodology:**
The research was conducted using first-principles calculations within the framework of Density Functional Theory (DFT) for ground-state properties and structural relaxation. To accurately describe the excited-state properties, Many-Body Perturbation Theory (MBPT) was employed, specifically using the $G_0W_0$ approximation for quasiparticle energies and the Bethe-Salpeter Equation (BSE) to include excitonic effects in the optical response.
