Defect engineering and functionalization enable the precise modulation of a material's local reactivity, electronic doping, and optical response. In this work, we show that the electronic properties of the 2H-phase $\text{TiBr}_2$ monolayer can be effectively tuned via vacancy engineering and adatom functionalization, thereby unlocking potential applications in catalysis, sensing, and optoelectronic devices.

**Key Findings:**
* **Pristine Surface**: For the pristine surface, all tested adatoms (H, C, N, and O) exhibit weak physisorption, with adsorption energies $|E_{ads}| < 1 \text{ eV}$.
* **Vacancy-Enhanced Reactivity**: The introduction of Br vacancies dramatically enhances the surface reactivity. This results in strong chemisorption at the vacancy sites for all adatoms, accompanied by large adsorption energies and substantial electronic modifications.
* **Property Tuning**: The findings demonstrate that the electronic and adsorptive properties of $\text{TiBr}_2$ monolayers can be rationally engineered through defect and adatom manipulation.

**Methodology:**
The study utilized density functional theory (DFT) to investigate pristine and modified monolayers, including Br-type vacancies and functionalization by common atomic adsorbates. A comprehensive energetic decomposition framework (adsorption, interaction, and distortion energies) was applied to the transition-metal dihalide monolayers.
