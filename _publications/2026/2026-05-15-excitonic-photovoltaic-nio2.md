Developing new two-dimensional materials for photovoltaics is a central strategy to address the world's growing energy demands. In this study, we present a comprehensive characterization of the $1\text{T-NiO}_2$ monolayer, exploring its potential for next-generation optoelectronic devices.

**Key Findings:**
* **Stability and Vibrations**: The $1\text{T-NiO}_2$ monolayer demonstrates structural stability, confirmed through the analysis of vibrational modes and its calculated Raman spectrum.
* **Excitonic Effects**: The material exhibits strong excitonic effects, with a significant exciton binding energy of approximately 800 meV, which is crucial for its optical performance.
* **Electronic Structure**: Using advanced computational methods, we accurately captured the quasiparticle properties, revealing a electronic structure highly suitable for solar energy conversion.
* **Mechanical Properties**: The study also evaluates the mechanical integrity of the monolayer, ensuring its viability for practical applications in flexible electronics.

**Methodology:**
The investigation was conducted through a multilevel approach using first-principles calculations based on Density Functional Theory (DFT) for ground-state properties. This was complemented by Many-Body Perturbation Theory (MBPT), employing the $G_0W_0$ approximation to determine quasiparticle energies and the Bethe-Salpeter Equation (BSE) to account for electron-hole interactions and optical spectra.
