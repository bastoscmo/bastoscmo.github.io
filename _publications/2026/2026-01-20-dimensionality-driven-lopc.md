The recent synthesis of long-range ordered porous carbon (LOPC) from $C_{60}$ opens new avenues for exploring fullerene-derived crystalline carbons with unconventional electronic behavior. In this work, we employ first-principles density functional theory (DFT) and many-body perturbation theory to investigate LOPC in one-, two-, and three-dimensional architectures.

**Key Findings:**
* **Electronic Behavior**: 1D and 2D LOPC are identified as small-gap semiconductors with indirect band gaps of 0.17 eV and 0.11 eV, respectively.
* **Dimensionality Effects**: The study reveals how the transition from 1D to 3D structures significantly alters the electronic and excitonic properties of the material.
* **Optical Properties**: We provide a detailed analysis of the excitonic effects and optical absorption spectra across different dimensionalities.

**Methodology:**
The investigation was conducted using Density Functional Theory (DFT) combined with many-body perturbation theory (BSE) to accurately describe the electronic and excitonic states.
