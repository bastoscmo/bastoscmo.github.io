The biphenylene monolayer (BPN) is a recently synthesized nonbenzenoid 2D carbon allotrope exhibiting metallic electronic transport in its quasi-one-dimensional nanoribbon form, while its boron nitride analogue (BPN-BN) is a wide-band-gap insulator. This work theoretically designs atomically sharp in-plane BPN/BPN-BN heterojunction nanoribbons with armchair and zigzag edge orientations and investigates their structural stability, electronic structure, and excitonic optical response using first-principles calculations.

**Key Findings:**
* **Electronic Structure**: Armchair configurations exhibit moderate, tunable band gaps between 1.3 and 1.5 eV, while zigzag heterojunctions display narrow-gap characteristics.
* **Excitonic Effects**: Many-body calculations reveal substantial exciton binding energies exceeding 0.20 eV, reflecting reduced dielectric screening in these low-dimensional systems.
* **Excitonic Instability**: Zigzag heterojunctions show signatures of excitonic-insulator-like behavior, driven by enhanced Coulomb interactions under quasi-one-dimensional confinement.

**Methodology:**
The structural, electronic, and excitonic properties of the BPN/BPN-BN in-plane heterojunction nanoribbons were evaluated through first-principles density functional theory combined with many-body perturbation theory.
