Two-dimensional boron-carbon-nitrogen (BCN) materials represent a versatile class of layered compounds that bridge the electronic and structural characteristics of graphene and hexagonal boron nitride. In this work, we report a first-principles investigation of $BC_2N$ monolayers, exploring eleven possible atomic configurations.

**Key Findings:**
* **Stability**: Six out of the eleven designed structures demonstrated dynamical and thermal stability, confirmed through phonon dispersion and ab initio molecular dynamics simulations.
* **Mechanical Properties**: The stable monolayers exhibit high in-plane stiffness, with elastic constants ranging from 252 to 305 N/m, Young’s moduli between 743 and 844 GPa, and ultimate tensile strengths reaching up to 104 GPa.
* **Electronic and Optical Structure**: While one configuration shows metallic behavior, the others possess direct band gaps ranging from 0.29 to 2.71 eV (calculated with the HSE06 functional). Exciton binding energies vary from 134 to 471 meV.
* **Photovoltaic Performance**: The Spectroscopic Limited Maximum Efficiency (SLME) analysis reveals promising performance, with specific structural phases reaching efficiencies of 20.5% and 31.5%.

**Methodology:**
The study employed density functional theory (DFT) using the SIESTA code for structural optimization and molecular dynamics. The HSE06 hybrid functional was used to correct band gap underestimation. Optical properties were obtained via the independent particle approximation (IPA) and the Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE) using the WanTiBEXOS code.
