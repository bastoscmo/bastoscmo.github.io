This work employs first-principles calculations to examine how structural symmetry tunes excitons in lead- and tin-iodide perovskites across three-dimensional, quasi-two-dimensional, and model quasi-one-dimensional configurations.

**Key Findings:**
* **Exciton Binding**: Increasing structural symmetry reduces exciton binding energy across the perovskite series studied.
* **Rashba Suppression**: Restoring metal-centered inversion symmetry suppresses Rashba spin splitting.
* **Underlying Mechanism**: These effects act primarily through modifications of the band-edge structure, rather than through direct strengthening of the Coulomb interaction kernel.

**Methodology:**
The study combined first-principles density functional theory, including spin-orbit coupling, with many-body perturbation theory to correlate structural symmetry, Rashba splitting, and excitonic properties across the perovskite dimensionality series.
