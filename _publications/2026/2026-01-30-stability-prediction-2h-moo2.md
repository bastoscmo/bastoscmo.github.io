The conception and development of two-dimensional (2D) materials have significantly advanced the design of next-generation optoelectronic devices. [cite_start]This work comprehensively investigates the $\text{MoO}_2$ monolayer in its 2H phase[cite: 932, 937].

**Key Findings:**
* [cite_start]**Stability**: The structural stability of the monolayer is robustly confirmed at dynamical (phonons), thermodynamic (AIMD), and mechanical (elastic constants) levels[cite: 932, 937].
* [cite_start]**Electronic Structure**: The material exhibits semiconducting behavior with a direct band gap of 2.50 eV (at the HSE06 level) and weak spin-orbit coupling, along with spin degeneracy breaking in the valence bands near the Fermi level[cite: 932, 937].
* [cite_start]**Excitonic Effects**: Analysis revealed a binding energy of 0.38 eV, causing significant excitonic effects in the linear optical response, which remains isotropic across the infrared and visible ranges[cite: 932, 937].

**Methodology:**
The research employed density functional theory (DFT) using PBE and HSE06 functionals. [cite_start]Optical and excitonic properties were derived by solving the Bethe-Salpeter equation (BSE), supported by phonon, AIMD, and elastic constant calculations[cite: 932, 937].
