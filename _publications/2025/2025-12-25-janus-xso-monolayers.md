The urgent global demand for clean and efficient energy has intensified the search for novel low-dimensional materials with photovoltaic potential. In this work, we investigate the electronic structure and solar harvesting efficiency of Janus XSO (X = Sn, Ge) monolayers.

**Key Findings:**
* **Electronic Properties**: The Janus XSO monolayers exhibit semiconducting characteristics with band gaps ranging from 2.35 to 3.28 eV at the HSE06 level.
* **Excitonic Effects**: The study incorporates many-body effects to accurately describe the excitonic properties and optical absorption spectra.
* **Solar Efficiency**: Our results highlight the potential of these materials for high-efficiency solar energy conversion, driven by their unique broken inversion symmetry.

**Methodology:**
The investigation was conducted using first-principles Density Functional Theory (DFT) combined with the Bethe-Salpeter Equation (BSE) to account for electron-hole interactions and provide a realistic assessment of the light-harvesting efficiency.
